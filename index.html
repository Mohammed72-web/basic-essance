Pillar 01

Sleep

Most people think the gym is where muscle is built. It's not. The gym is where you tear muscle down. Sleep is where it grows back — bigger and stronger than before.

When you train, you create millions of microscopic tears in your muscle fibers. During deep sleep, your body releases Growth Hormone, floods the damaged tissue with amino acids, and repairs every one of those tears. The result? Slightly more muscle than you had before. That's the entire mechanism of muscle growth.

Now here's what destroys it: inconsistent sleep timing. It's not just about hours — it's about schedule. Sleeping at 8pm one night, 3am the next, then 11am ruins your hormonal rhythm entirely.

⚠ What happens with bad sleep

Cortisol rises · Testosterone drops · Growth Hormone drops · Protein synthesis slows · Fat gain increases · Workouts suffer

How to Fix Your Sleep Schedule

  • 1
    Morning sunlight. Get outside within an hour of waking — no shirt, direct sun for at least 30 minutes. This tells your brain it's daytime and anchors your internal clock.
  • 2
    Wake up at the same time every day. Even if you slept badly. Do not nap. Your body will consolidate sleep naturally within a few days.
  • 3
    No phone 1 hour before bed — and not just because of blue light. The real issue is dopamine. Scrolling keeps your brain stimulated and kills your ability to fall asleep. Read, pray, meditate, organize your space — anything away from a screen.
Understanding Your Body

The Hormones

Three hormones control most of what happens in your body as a bodybuilder. Know them. Respect them.

Testosterone

The primary muscle-building hormone. It boosts the rate your muscle cells build new proteins, lowers muscle breakdown, and makes you stronger so you can create more stimulus. Think of it as the construction manager on your job site.

IGF-1

Released by muscle cells after training — not by the brain like Growth Hormone. IGF-1 tells your muscle cells to grab amino acids and use them as bricks to repair and grow. GH and IGF-1 are not the same thing.

Cortisol

The enemy. It breaks down muscle tissue and slows recovery. It rises when you wake up, when you're in a bad mood, and when you train for more than an hour. Keep it low through consistent sleep, calm mornings, and controlled training sessions.

"Amino acids = bricks. Testosterone & GH = construction managers. Your DNA = the blueprint."
Pillar 02

Nutrition

Food is your raw material. Without it, there is nothing to build with. Here's what actually happens after you eat: food travels from your mouth → esophagus → stomach (where acid breaks it down) → small intestine (where it's absorbed) → liver (which filters it) → bloodstream. Simple as that.

The 3 Macronutrients

🍚
Carbs
Bodyweight × 6 = grams/day
Stored as glycogen in muscles. Makes them look fuller and provides training energy. Carbs are not the enemy — sugar is.
🥩
Protein
Bodyweight × 2 = grams/day
The bricks of muscle. You need multiple sources — eggs, red meat, sardines — because each provides different amino acids your body requires.
🥑
Good Fat
Eggs, nuts, olive oil
Fat is what your body uses to produce testosterone and other hormones. Egg yolks + mixed nuts before bed = a simple hormone boost.
💡 Protein tip

If you're on a budget: canned sardines and chicken legs are cheap, high-quality protein sources. Most people throw the legs away — that's free gains.

Eating Schedule

Eat every 2–3 hours. Keep meals small — never eat until you feel full. Your goal is to maintain a steady supply of nutrients without overwhelming your digestive system. If you go 4–5 hours without eating, your body starts burning muscle for energy.

Water

Aim for 1–2 gallons a day. Sip consistently — don't chug large amounts at once. Spread it across the whole day.

🔄 Gut Reset — Every 3 Months

Fast for 2 weeks every 3 months to reset your gut health. Keep eating protein and minerals. Do light cardio. Your digestive system handles 6+ meals a day, which is evolutionary unusual — give it a break regularly.

Pillar 03

Training

Every exercise exists for one reason: to apply stress to a specific muscle in a specific direction, aligned with its fiber orientation. Your chest needs to be stretched and pressed. Your biceps need to be curled. The muscle fiber direction determines which exercise actually works that muscle.

The 2 Basics: Squeeze & Stretch

Squeeze (pump): A squeezing exercise floods the muscle with blood, forces it to flex so no other muscle takes over, and pumps oxygen into the tissue. The result is a harder, better-isolated contraction and more even fiber recruitment.

Stretch: After the muscle is warm and pumped, a stretch exercise creates deep fiber damage at the muscle's full extension — that's where most growth happens. Tom Platz and Arnold both built their best body parts using this principle.

🧠 The Formula

Always squeeze first — warm the muscle, get the blood in, activate the fibers. Then stretch. Never stretch a cold muscle.

The Breakdown

Muscle by Muscle

Click each muscle to expand the training guide.

Chest

+

The chest has three regions: upper, middle, and lower. Most beginners skip the middle and go straight for upper chest — this leads to shoulder injury because you don't have the base muscle to support it.

Start with the middle chest to build the foundation. Lower chest takes care of itself as your chest develops forward and down.

Beginner: 2 exercises — 1 squeeze (bench press or machine press), 1 stretch (dips).

Intermediate: 3 exercises — 2 squeeze (flat + incline), 1 stretch (dips or flyes).

Advanced: 5 exercises — full arsenal targeting upper, mid, lower, and detail work.

Key exercises: Bench press · Incline dumbbell press · Dumbbell flyes · Pullovers · Dips

Reps: Beginner 12/10/8 · Intermediate 10–12 steady

Shoulders

+

Shoulders are the second largest muscle group and one of the hardest to develop. A well-developed shoulder makes your chest, lats, and triceps all look more defined.

Always train back shoulder first, then side, then front. If you start with side shoulder you'll waste energy before you hit the parts that matter most.

Shoulders need 20+ reps — they're an evolutionary endurance muscle. High volume, high frequency. They deserve their own dedicated day.

⚠ Never pair shoulders with chest or back

The joint stress combination will lead to injury. Shoulders alone, or with a small muscle like traps or biceps.

Key exercises: Dumbbell press · Front raises · Seated side laterals · Standing laterals · Wide-grip upright rows · Rear delt flyes · Pec deck (rear)

Biceps & Triceps

+

Triceps are 70% of your arm. If you want arms that fill a shirt, train triceps — not biceps. Most people have this backwards.

Train arms twice a week: first session is 3 tricep exercises + 2 bicep. Second session is 1 tricep + 1 bicep. Full range of motion, every rep.

Tricep exercises: Pushdowns · Skull crushers · Tricep dips (feel the stretch in the front shoulder and tricep, not the chest)

Bicep tip: Most people overtrain biceps and under-feel them. Use techniques like 3-second pauses, slow negatives, and drop sets. The best bicep exercise is chin-ups or close-grip pulldowns — they squeeze AND stretch the muscle in one movement.

The preacher curl is the best isolated stretch for biceps. Don't be afraid of full range of motion — just use appropriate weight.

Back

+

The "back" most people see in photos is actually the lats — but that's only about 35% of the full back. The other 35%+ is traps (upper, middle, lower). Both need specific work.

The #1 back mistake: Not feeling your lats. If you don't feel them, you're pulling with your arms. Fix it with one phrase: "Pull with your lats, not your biceps."

Close your eyes if it helps. Imagine the lats pulling, not the hands. Slow 2-second positives, 3–4 second negatives. 4–5 sets on rowing movements.

Lats exercises: Cable rows · Barbell rows · Pull-ups · Lat pulldowns

Traps: 6 total sets. Shrugs (3 sets regular, 1 heavy drop set with 3-second squeeze). Also 45-degree bench shrugs for mid/lower traps. Reps 10–14.

Tip: Practice flexing your lats throughout the day — walking, sitting, anywhere. This builds the mind-muscle connection faster than any exercise alone.

Legs

+

Legs are the biggest muscle group. Train them with the same intensity as shoulders — which is extreme. Tom Platz's leg training is your benchmark.

Always start with leg extension. Your quads are not naturally sensitive muscles — they need to be pre-activated before squats or lunges, otherwise the stress goes to your knees instead of the muscle.

Quads: 15–30 reps, 5+ sets per exercise. Legs keep recruiting new fibers as old ones fatigue — that's why one set never feels like enough. Squeeze: leg extension. Stretch: squats, lunges.

Hamstrings: Leg curls (squeeze) + Romanian deadlift (stretch). Push your glutes back as far as possible in the deadlift — this fully stretches the hamstring without loading the lower back.

Calves: 1 exercise — standing calf raises. Medium weight, 12–14 reps, squeeze at the top. On the final set go near maximum weight, hold until failure, then bounce into a deep stretch. Calves aren't stubborn — they're just undertrained at full range.

The Final Chapter

Fat Loss

Fat is an energy reserve — stored by your body for times of scarcity. In the modern world we run almost entirely on carbohydrates, so fat rarely gets touched. To burn fat, you have to make your body need it.

How Fat Burning Works

When you cut carbs and sugar significantly, your body enters ketosis — it switches to burning fat as its primary fuel. This is not a fad. It's how your body actually works. You'll feel clearer, more energized, and you'll lose fat steadily.

A simple effective diet for fat loss: 3 boiled eggs with salt + 1 can of sardines + potassium supplement. Adjust from there, but that base hits protein, fat, and minerals.

Training During a Cut

When dieting, train with higher reps — push past 30 reps per set until the muscle burns. Keep going through the burn. This improves muscle definition because you're increasing the visible separation and hardness of the muscle underneath the fat. Note: this doesn't burn fat from a specific spot — fat loss is whole-body — but it does reveal the muscle definition underneath as your overall body fat drops.

⚠ Avoid during a cut

No heavy stretching exercises. No changing your mind mid-diet. Consistency is everything. Switching between bulk and cut every few weeks achieves nothing.

Muscle Memory

Don't be afraid of losing muscle during a cut. Muscle memory is real. Once you return to training and eating for growth, your body rebuilds lost muscle dramatically faster than it took to build it the first time — often in just 3–4 weeks.

"The best bodybuilder is not who you see on stage. The best bodybuilder is the one who makes bodybuilding a healthy lifestyle and looks great for decades."

For the Busy Person

No gym? No time? You don't need either to start. One rule: drink a full glass of cold water before every meal. Slowly cut sugar and bread over a month. Watch what happens. Progress is progress, no matter how small.
